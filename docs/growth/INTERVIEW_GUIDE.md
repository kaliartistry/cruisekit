# CruiseKit First 20 Interview Guide

Status: reusable research guide for voluntary post-onboarding and post-cruise interviews.

## Purpose

Learn which real pre-cruise and onboard problems CruiseKit solves, where people lose confidence, and what would make the product worth returning to or sharing. This is not a satisfaction survey or a sales call.

## Before the Interview

- Confirm the participant is comfortable with a 20–30 minute conversation.
- Explain that honest criticism is useful and participation is optional.
- Ask permission before recording. If permission is not given, take minimal factual notes only.
- Do not request booking references, payment data, exact cabin numbers, live location, or private crew conversations.
- Review the user’s protected internal timeline only to avoid asking questions already answered; do not expose it to anyone else.

## Suggested Flow

| Time | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Thank the participant, restate purpose, request recording consent if applicable. |
| 3–10 min | Their prior planning process and trigger for looking. |
| 10–18 min | Specific CruiseKit use, confusion, and valuable moments. |
| 18–24 min | Crew/referral behavior, alternatives, and next-cruise intent. |
| 24–30 min | Prioritized improvement, permission for follow-up, optional review request only after value. |

## Core Questions

Ask open questions first; use prompts only when needed.

1. Before CruiseKit, what did you use to plan or keep track of this cruise?
2. What happened that made you look for help at that moment?
3. What was your biggest pre-cruise anxiety or uncertainty?
4. Before you sailed, what did you expect the trip to cost? What did it actually cost, as far as you can tell?
5. Walk me through the first time you used CruiseKit. What did you expect to happen?
6. Which parts of CruiseKit were confusing, slow, missing, or hard to trust?
7. What was the single most valuable moment, if any?
8. Did you invite anyone to your crew or share the tool? Why or why not?
9. If you did not invite someone, what stopped you—timing, trust, usefulness, setup, privacy, or something else?
10. What would make you use CruiseKit on your next cruise?
11. What would you be disappointed to lose if CruiseKit disappeared tomorrow?

## Follow-up Prompts

- “Can you show me the last time that happened?”
- “What did you do instead?”
- “What made that feel risky or confusing?”
- “Was that a one-time issue or something you expect every cruise?”
- “If you could change one thing first, what would it be?”

Avoid asking “Did you like it?” before obtaining concrete behavior. Do not defend the product; ask what happened.

## Closing

1. Reflect back the top one or two problems in the participant’s language.
2. Ask whether a follow-up is welcome if the reported issue changes.
3. If value was clearly demonstrated and there is no unresolved issue, offer an optional, no-pressure review request separately.
4. Thank them and state that their information remains private.
