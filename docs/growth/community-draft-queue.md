## Draft 1 - Drink Package Math

Target pattern: "Is the drink package worth it?"

No-link answer angle:

> I would calculate it by cabin average, not by one person's best-case day. Multiply the daily package price by every adult who has to buy it, add service charge, then compare that against realistic drinks on sea days and port days separately. The port-day adjustment is where a lot of package math breaks.

## Draft 2 - Hidden Costs For First Cruise

Target pattern: "What should I know before booking my first cruise?"

No-link answer angle:

> The big surprise is usually the stack of small costs: taxes/port fees, daily gratuities, WiFi, drinks, excursions, parking, hotels, insurance, and port cash. I would price the trip as fare plus mandatory fees plus the extras you are realistically going to buy, then compare cruise lines on that total.

Link status: no link.

## Draft 3 - How Much Cash To Bring

Target pattern: "How much cash should we bring?"

No-link answer angle:

> The ship itself is mostly cashless, but ports are the reason to carry small bills. I would think in buckets: taxi/transport, extra tips, beach vendors, small food/drink stops, and backup money if a card terminal is down.

Link status: no link.

## Draft 4 - NCL Free At Sea

Target pattern: "Is Free at Sea actually free?"

No-link answer angle:

> I would treat Free at Sea as a bundle, not as free. The value can be real, but the fare, gratuities, WiFi limits, specialty dining rules, and upgrade costs all need to be compared against what you would buy separately on another line.

Link status: no link.

## Draft 5 - Carnival CHEERS

Target pattern: "Is Carnival CHEERS worth it?"

No-link answer angle:

> The cabin rule is the part to model first. If every adult in the stateroom has to buy it, the question becomes whether the average adult in the room drinks enough every day to beat the total package cost.

## Draft 6 - Disney Family Budget

Target pattern: "How much should I budget for Disney Cruise Line?"

No-link answer angle:

> For Disney, I would budget by family total: fare, taxes, gratuities for every guest, WiFi devices, adult dining, excursions, photos, hotel/transportation, and port spending. The included entertainment is strong, but WiFi and excursions still move the number quickly.

Link status: no link.

## Draft 7 - Cruise WiFi

Target pattern: "Is cruise WiFi worth it?"

No-link answer angle:

> Price WiFi by what you actually need: messaging, email, or streaming/work calls. Then multiply by devices and nights. One-device messaging math looks very different from two phones plus a laptop for seven nights.

Link status: no link.
